About WinterRoad & Research Team

System design, methodological assumptions, technical limitations, and open-source licensing.

Research Team

WinterBorn

Authors: B K Kesavkrishnan · S Arun Kumar

WinterRoad bridges automated defect computer vision and civil engineering operational research. It replaces arbitrary subjective road repair choices with a verifiable, mathematically provable optimization model that maximizes road safety under constrained civic budgets.

Methodological Assumptions

  • 2D Severity Proxy: Camera images capture bounding box area (\(\%\) of image frame) and damage category, serving as a 2D surface proxy. True cavity depth is not measured without stereoscopic sensors or LiDAR.
  • Unit Cost Schedule: Repair costs are estimated using standardized municipal PWD unit rates (e.g., ₹3,000 for D00 crack sealing, ₹6,000 for D40 pothole patch, ₹15,000 for D20 structural overlay).
  • Traffic Exposure Weights: Traffic multiplier PCU weights (\(1.0\) for primary arterial corridors, \(0.7\) for secondary/tertiary roads, \(0.4\) for residential streets) represent relative public exposure.
  • Proximity Bonuses: Defects within 500m of hospitals receive up to a \(+60\%\) risk bonus to protect critical emergency response corridors.

Known Limitations & Future Scope

  • Lighting and Weather Variations: High-noon shadows, rain puddles, and glare can impact bounding box area estimation.
  • GPS Drift: Urban canyoning in high-rise corridors may introduce GPS inaccuracies of 10–20 meters.
  • Hardware Roadmap: Future iterations will integrate edge-device inference on low-power municipal patrol vehicles and depth sensing.
